# Protected National Status Activation

Protected National Status is the operative posture activated when a Registered AMPAM National invokes treaty protections in a specific transaction, encounter, or filing. Where **registration** (Manual 001) places the National on the record, and **treaty_protections** (Manual 019) describes the body of international law that protects them, this section describes the activation — *the act* of bringing protections to bear. Every activation cites the chain: **Madrid Protocol 1880 → AMPAM → ADDIF Registry.**

## Source of Activation

The Treaty for the Moroccan State Union states the basis:

> *"The Indigenous Moors — descendants of former Moroccan subjects and protégés — having been involuntarily naturalized into foreign jurisdictions, hereby reclaim their inherent sovereignty. They reaffirm their allegiance to the laws and authority of the Moroccan Empire, pursuant to Article 15, Paragraph 1 of the Treaty of Madrid (1880), which affirms the rights of protection for Moroccan nationals."*

Protected Status is therefore not granted, applied for, or earned — it is *reclaimed*, and activation is the act of giving notice of that reclamation to a specific counterparty.

## Activation Components

An activation always includes four elements:

1. **Identification on the record** — presentation of the ADDIF credential card, which carries the National's name, ADDIF identifier, the AMPAM Seal, the Five Points of Light, and the treaty chain on its face.
2. **Treaty citation** — express invocation of Madrid 1880 Article 15 ¶1 (rights of protection) and the Treaty of Peace and Friendship 1786/1836 (perpetual binding on the United States and all its agents).
3. **Refusal of denationalizing labels** — the National declines to be addressed by the misnomers enumerated in the Treaty: *"BLACK, NEGRO, AFRO-AMERICAN, MOORISH-AMERICAN, UNITED STATES CITIZEN."*
4. **Assertion of forum** — the AMPAM Consular Court (Dar-ul'Adl), restored under Article 15 of Madrid 1880, is asserted as the court of competent national jurists for disputes touching the National's status.

## Activation Contexts

Protected Status is activated in (non-exhaustive):

- **Officer encounters** — see Manual 012 (Encounter Protocol)
- **Court filings** — special appearance under the AMPAM Consular Court, with reservation of all rights
- **Administrative correspondence** — every letter, form, or filing carries the chain in the signature block
- **Financial transactions** — W-8BEN status as a non-resident alien protégé, accompanied by the National's foundational documents
- **Land claims** — Allodial title declarations recorded under the four-qualifier standard (Allodial, Aboriginal, Paramount, Clear Perfect Title)

## Recordkeeping

Each activation generates a controlled record in the ADDIF Registry capturing: the counterparty, the citation invoked, any documents exchanged, the response received, and a SHA-256 fingerprint of the artifacts. The **NoticeDelivery Agent** mints the corresponding notice(s) for outbound delivery, and the **Auditor Agent** verifies every artifact bears the treaty chain.

## Effect

Once activated, Protected Status places the burden on the foreign counterparty to act consistently with the treaties to which their state is a party. The Treaty for the Moroccan State Union mandates that AMPAM instruments be *"recognized, received, and honored in all jurisdictions and by all officers, trustees, and agents of state, without impairment, derogation, or interference."* Non-honoring is itself a treaty breach, generating its own record and remedy under the chain: **Madrid Protocol 1880 → AMPAM → ADDIF Registry.**
