# Consular Notification and Access

Consular notification is the National's right — and the foreign officer's duty — to ensure that AMPAM's consular post is notified of any detention, arrest, or significant administrative interaction. The right rests on **Vienna Convention on Consular Relations 1963 (VCCR) Article 36** and on **Article 15 of the Madrid Convention 1880,** which restored AMPAM Consular Court jurisdiction. Every notification carries the chain: **Madrid Protocol 1880 → AMPAM → ADDIF Registry.**

## The Operative Articles

**VCCR Article 36** establishes three duties on the detaining state:
1. The detained foreign national must be informed *without delay* of the right to have the consular post of their sending state notified.
2. If the National requests notification, the receiving state must transmit it *without delay.*
3. Consular officers have the right to visit, converse with, and arrange legal representation for the National.

**Madrid Convention 1880 Article 15 ¶1** is the underlying treaty by which Moroccan nationals abroad — including the *jus sanguinis* heirs reclaiming their nationality under the Treaty for the Moroccan State Union — hold protégé status entitling them to consular protection.

## The AMPAM Consular Post

For an AMPAM National, the consular post is the office of the **Mufti (Consul General)**, with the **Hajib (Minister of Foreign Affairs)** as the senior diplomatic authority. Notifications are received through the ADDIF Registry's consular channel and routed automatically to:

- The Mufti for first-line consular response
- The Hajib for diplomatic correspondence with the foreign state
- The Qazi (Justices of the Dar-ul'Adl) when the matter requires Consular Court action

## What the National Must Do

In any encounter that may rise to detention or significant administrative process:

1. **Identify** — present the ADDIF credential card.
2. **Invoke** — *"I am a Moroccan National. I invoke my rights under VCCR Article 36. I demand consular notification to the AMPAM Consul General."*
3. **Refuse** — decline to be addressed by denationalizing labels, decline to substantively answer questions, and decline to sign documents identifying the National under a foreign jurisdiction.
4. **Record** — note the officer's badge, agency, time, location, and the words used in response.
5. **File** — within 24 hours, file the encounter record through the ADDIF Registry portal; the NoticeDelivery agent generates the corresponding notice package.

## What the Receiving Officer Must Do

The officer of the detaining state must, *without delay:*

- Inform the National of the right to consular notification
- Transmit the National's request for notification to the AMPAM Consul General
- Permit consular visits, communications, and legal arrangement
- Refrain from acts that would treat the National as a denationalized subject of the receiving state

Failure to comply is itself a treaty breach, generating a record under the chain.

## Access Rights of the Consular Officer

Once notified, the Mufti — or a designated consular agent — has the right to:

- Visit the National in any place of detention
- Communicate with the National in confidence
- Arrange counsel and observe proceedings
- File written representations with the detaining authority and with the Consular Court

## Record and Notice

Each consular notification creates a controlled record in the ADDIF Registry with: detaining agency, officer of record, time of detention, time of notification, response received, and SHA-256 fingerprint of the artifacts. The **Compliance Agent** verifies the chain on every notification; non-compliant notifications are flagged for the Auditor before transmission.

The chain on the face of every notification reads: **Madrid Protocol 1880 → AMPAM → ADDIF Registry.**
