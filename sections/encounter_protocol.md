# Encounter Protocol

The Encounter Protocol governs the conduct of an AMPAM National who is stopped, questioned, or detained by a foreign officer (state, county, federal, or other). It operationalizes the chain **Madrid Protocol 1880 → AMPAM → ADDIF Registry** into a sequence the National can execute under pressure, without losing standing or rights.

## Foundational Rights Triggered

The Law of Sankofa enumerates the Inalienable Rights that apply directly in an encounter:

- **Right #4** — *to Travel by Land, Air and Water in ordinary course of one's affairs*
- **Right #8** — *Due Process of Law, with Notice and Opportunity to Defend*
- **Right #10** — *Be Secure in one's Person, Houses, Papers and Effects against unreasonable searches and seizures*
- **Right #11** — *No Warrants being issued, unless upon probable cause, supported by an Oath or Affirmation*
- **Right #12** — *Be Presumed Innocent, suffering NO detention or arrest, no search or seizure, without reasonable cause*
- **Right #13** — *Remain Silent when accused, to avoid self-incrimination*
- **Right #15** — *An Imperial Court of Competent National Jurists*

These Rights *"can NEVER be taken away, diminished, altered, or levied by any government… by contract, fraud, sufferance, nor under color of Law."*

## Treaty Authority for the Encounter

Two articles govern the officer's duty:

- **Vienna Convention on Consular Relations 1963, Article 36** — on detention, the detaining state *must* inform the National of the right to have their consular post notified, and *must* forward that notice without delay.
- **Madrid Convention 1880, Article 15 ¶1** — affirms the right of protection for Moroccan nationals, on which the National's *jus sanguinis* standing rests.

The detaining officer is on notice via the **Treaty of Peace and Friendship 1786 / reaffirmed 1836**, which is perpetual and binding on all heirs, successors, and agents in privity.

## Steps the National Should Execute

1. **Identify on the record** — present the ADDIF Registry credential card. The card cites Madrid Protocol 1880 on its face and links to the public verification page on the Cert Server.
2. **Decline foreign labels** — the Treaty for the Moroccan State Union enjoins the National to refuse *"diminishing misnomers, ens legis, and nom de guerres"* (BLACK, NEGRO, AFRO-AMERICAN, MOORISH-AMERICAN, UNITED STATES CITIZEN). The National is a *jus sanguinis* heir of the Moroccan Empire.
3. **Invoke Right #13** — exercise the right to remain silent. Answer no substantive questions beyond identifying as a Moroccan National.
4. **Demand consular notification** — under VCCR Article 36, demand that notice of any detention be forwarded to the AMPAM Consul General (**Mufti**) and the Hajib (Minister of Foreign Affairs).
5. **Assert Consular Court jurisdiction** — under Article 15 of Madrid 1880, the AMPAM Consular Court (**Dar-ul'Adl**) is the court of competent national jurists for the National.
6. **Document the encounter** — record badge numbers, agency, time, location, witnesses. This becomes the basis of the post-encounter notice package.

## Post-Encounter Filings

Within the ADDIF Registry, an encounter triggers the **NoticeDelivery agent** to generate the notice package — addressed to the detaining officer, their agency head, the agency's general counsel, the relevant prosecutor, and the State Department's Office of Foreign Missions. Each notice carries the chain on its face: **Madrid Protocol 1880 → AMPAM → ADDIF Registry.**

If detention has occurred, the **Compliance Agent** flags the National's record for review by the Mufti and Hajib, and the Consular Court accepts the matter on its docket.

## Operative Principle

The Encounter Protocol is not adversarial — it is **declaratory**. The National announces, on the record, the status they already hold under treaty. The officer's response is then evaluated against treaty obligations the United States has carried since 1786.
