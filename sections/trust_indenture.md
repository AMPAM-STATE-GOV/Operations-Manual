# Trust Indenture and Land Ante Title Restoration

This section sets out the use of trust indentures to restore Land Ante Title for AMPAM Nationals — that is, to formally re-document allodial title to ancestral land within the AMPAM Dominions or to assets sited in foreign jurisdictions. Every indenture carries the chain on its face: **Madrid Protocol 1880 → AMPAM → ADDIF Registry.**

## The Authority Chain for Title Restoration

The Treaty for the Moroccan State Union expressly mandates the Nationals to *"reclaim their allodial rights and ancestral inheritance"* and to *"make all reversionary claims regarding Moroccan Estates and the ancestral hereditaments thereby, both corporeal and incorporeal, in all jurisdictions."* Restoration is therefore not a permission sought from a foreign jurisdiction — it is a record made under AMPAM's own authority, asserted under treaty.

## The National Trust as Foundation

The Law of Sankofa establishes the **National Trust** as *"an International, Divinely Established, Sovereign, Allodial, Express Trust"* — first lien holder on all Lands claimed Allodial by the government and by Moroccan Nationals. Every individual indenture sits beneath, and is consistent with, this overarching trust.

## Components of a Trust Indenture

A National's trust indenture must include:

1. **Settlor** — the National in their *jus sanguinis* capacity, the natural person
2. **Trustee(s)** — Matrilineal Nationals (and/or the State National Trust); the Constitution permits three Trustees for matters of significant estate
3. **Beneficiary** — the National in their fiduciary/heir capacity; secondary beneficiaries may be designated for *jus sanguinis* heirs
4. **Corpus** — the assets brought into the trust: Land claims, financial assets, foundational documents, prior corporate forms
5. **Powers and limitations** — what the Trustee may and may not do; conformance with the Five Points of Light
6. **Reversion clause** — restoration of corpus to *jus sanguinis* heirs on the death of the National
7. **Citation block** — Treaty 1786/1836, Madrid 1880 Art. 15 ¶1, Algeciras 1906, and the chain

## Title Restoration Sequence

For Land within the AMPAM Dominions (Lat 30.5°–34.9° N, Long 80.8°–85.6° W):

1. **Locate the parcel** — by metes and bounds, GPS, and any prior recording
2. **Document the claim** — under the four-qualifier standard: Allodial, Aboriginal, Paramount, Clear Perfect Title
3. **Convey to the indenture** — record the parcel as corpus of the trust
4. **File with the Cadi** (Deed Tax Assessor) — the Cadi records the conveyance in the Registry's land tables
5. **Notice the Land Bank** — for accounting and any administrative oversight
6. **Mint the title NFT** — the `ALDRegistry` contract records the title under the domain namespace; the National holds the controlling key
7. **Issue the notice package** — see Manual 013 for the standard notices

For assets in foreign jurisdictions:

- A **Foreign Grantor Trust** structure may be used as the conduit, preserving the National's *jus sanguinis* standing while operating through forms recognized by the foreign jurisdiction
- The indenture references the underlying AMPAM trust as the senior instrument

## Recordkeeping

Every indenture is a controlled record. The artifact (signed PDF) is stored in `nmf_documents`; the metadata is in `foundational_documents`; on-chain references are in `nmf_events`. A SHA-256 hash anchors the artifact; on-chain entries provide immutable timestamping.

## Compliance and Audit

The Auditor Agent verifies, on each indenture: that the chain is on the face, that the citations match the source treaties, and that the trust structure is internally consistent. The Compliance Agent flags expired or superseded indentures and triggers renewal notices.
