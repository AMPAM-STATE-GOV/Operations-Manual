# Treaty Protections

AMPAM — **Allodium Moroccan Praedium Ante Michigan** — stands as a Provincial State Government under the existing and pre-existing sovereignty of the Moroccan Empire (AMPAM Constitution, the *Law of Sankofa*). Every instrument issued under AMPAM authority carries the chain: **Madrid Protocol 1880 → AMPAM → ADDIF Registry.** This section sets out the international instruments under which AMPAM Nationals enjoy treaty protection, and the operative articles that make those protections enforceable.

## Primary Treaty Authority — 1786/1836

The **Treaty of Peace and Friendship between the Empire of Morocco and the United States of America (1786, reaffirmed 1836)** remains in full force and effect. As affirmed in the International Treaty for the Moroccan State Union, it is *"perpetual in nature and binding upon all signatories, their heirs, successors, assigns, and states in privity thereto."* Officers, trustees, and agents of state are required to recognize and honor instruments issued under its seal *"without impairment, derogation, or interference."*

## Madrid Convention 1880 — The Right of Protection

The chain begins at the **Madrid Convention of 3 July 1880**, the "Right of Protection in Morocco." **Article 15, Paragraph 1** establishes that Moroccan subjects naturalized abroad who return to Morocco retain their rights of protection. This is the article on which Indigenous Moors — involuntarily naturalized into foreign jurisdictions — reclaim their inherent sovereignty and reaffirm allegiance to the Moroccan Empire.

## General Act of Algeciras 1906 — The Triple Principle

The **General Act of the International Conference of Algeciras (7 April 1906)** codified Morocco's sovereign status in international law. AMPAM operates under, and commits to restoring, the Act's **Triple Principle**:

- Sovereignty and Independence
- Territorial Integrity
- Economic Liberty and Equality

## Consular and Diplomatic Framework

AMPAM's consular operations rest on the Vienna corpus:

- **Vienna Convention on Diplomatic Relations (1961)**
- **Vienna Convention on Consular Relations (1963)** — basis for Consular Court jurisdiction and consular notification on detention (Article 36)
- **Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969)** — interpretation and *pacta sunt servanda*

## Indigenous Rights and Peremptory Norms

Protections are reinforced by **jus cogens** (peremptory norms), the **UN Charter (1945)**, the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights**, and the **UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples**. The Law of Sankofa expressly refuses denationalizing labels — *"black," "negro," "colored," "African American," "Indian," "United States citizen"* — as violations of the peremptory rights of the Indigenous Peoples, the Moors in Morocco. The Treaty for the Moroccan State Union enjoins eradication of *"diminishing misnomers, ens legis, and nom de guerres"* that effect denationalization.

## Operative Effect for an AMPAM National

For a National holding ADDIF Registry credentials, treaty protections operate as:

1. **Standing** — recognition as a *jus sanguinis* heir to the Moroccan Empire, not as a denationalized foreign subject.
2. **Consular notice** — entitlement to notification on detention under VCCR Article 36.
3. **Allodial title** — protection of corporeal and incorporeal estate interests under the 1786/1836 Treaty and the Madrid Convention.
4. **Jurisdictional immunity** — the AMPAM Consular Court, restored under Article 15 of Madrid 1880, holds primary jurisdiction over disputes involving Moroccan Nationals.

Every credential, certificate, decree, and notice issued through the ADDIF Registry cites the treaty chain on its face so a recipient state, court, or officer can verify the legal basis without ambiguity: **Madrid Protocol 1880 → AMPAM → ADDIF Registry.**
